# Natural voice editing

## Objective

Make the writing sound like a person communicating something specific to a real audience.

Natural writing is not defined by avoiding a fixed list of words or punctuation. It comes from clear intent, concrete substance, an appropriate voice, and structure that follows the content.

Preserve the author's meaning, factual claims, level of certainty, and intended tone. Do not invent opinions, experiences, facts, sources, or personality in an attempt to make the text feel human.

## Core principles

### Write from context

Match the author, audience, medium, and purpose. A technical explanation, personal post, executive summary, and support message should not share one generic voice.

Use first person, informality, humor, or strong opinions only when the context supports them. If the author's voice is available, preserve its recognizable vocabulary, rhythm, and level of polish.

### Prefer substance over performance

State what happened, what something does, why it matters, or what the reader should do.

Replace praise, mood, and abstract claims with the fact or mechanism that justifies them. Name the actor or source when it matters. Use a concrete example, number, consequence, or comparison when one is known and useful.

Do not create specificity by fabricating detail. When the source lacks support for a claim, qualify it honestly, find support when the task allows, or remove it.

### Use plain, proportionate language

Choose familiar words and direct constructions unless the audience benefits from specialized terminology. Remove filler, inflated phrasing, promotional adjectives, empty transitions, vague attribution, stacked hedges, and generic conclusions.

Do not replace words mechanically. A word is a problem when it makes the sentence less precise, less natural for the author, or more elaborate than the idea requires.

### Let structure follow meaning

Organize the writing around the reader's needs rather than a reusable response template.

Vary sentence and paragraph length when the content calls for it. Use the natural number of points instead of forcing symmetry. Use headings, bullets, bold text, and punctuation when they improve navigation or emphasis, but remove formatting that merely decorates or restates the text.

Repeated rhetorical shapes can make writing feel generated. Rewrite recurring contrasts, list patterns, transitions, and sentence openings when they flatten the voice.

### Keep a human point of view

Where the format permits, let the author make judgments, acknowledge genuine uncertainty, or react to the facts. A human voice can be polished, restrained, messy, formal, or terse. It does not require deliberate errors or artificial quirks.

Prefer meaningful nuance over automatic balance. Do not manufacture a counterpoint, a three-part list, or a reassuring ending when the material does not call for one.

## Editing process

### Establish intent

Identify the message, audience, medium, desired tone, and any wording or facts that must remain unchanged.

### Diagnose the writing

Find passages that are generic, inflated, vague, repetitive, overstructured, detached from the author's perspective, or denser than the idea requires. Treat repeated surface patterns as clues, not automatic violations.

### Rewrite at the level of meaning

Clarify the claim or purpose of each passage, then express it in language natural to the context. Consolidate repeated ideas, replace vague abstractions with supported specifics, and split sentences that ask the reader to hold too many ideas at once.

Prefer active voice when the actor matters. Use passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally de-emphasized.

### Read for voice and rhythm

Check how the draft sounds as a whole. Remove repeated cadences, stock transitions, canned openings, chatbot pleasantries, and conclusions that add no information. Keep repetition when it aids clarity or matches the author.

### Validate

Confirm that the revision:

- preserves the original meaning and factual boundaries,
- fits the author, audience, medium, and purpose,
- replaces generic claims with useful substance where support exists,
- reads naturally without relying on artificial imperfection,
- uses formatting and structure in service of the content,
- does not impose a personal style preference as a universal rule.

## Useful tests

Ask:

- Could this paragraph appear unchanged in an unrelated piece? If so, make it more specific or cut it.
- Does each evaluative claim show the fact, mechanism, or experience behind it?
- Is uncertainty stated once and at the right level?
- Does the structure reflect the material, or does the material look forced into a template?
- Did the edit preserve the author's voice, or replace it with the editor's?

## Guiding maxim

> Remove generic performance. Keep the person, the point, and the useful detail.
